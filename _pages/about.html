Hi, I’m Brianna Wimer, a Ph.D candidate in Computer Science and Engineering at the University of Notre Dame, advised by Dr. Ron Metoyer. I live in Seattle, WA, where I work as a visiting researcher at the University of Washington, advised by Dr. Jen Mankoff.

I design and build systems that support accessible diagrams. My work sits at the intersection of accessibility and data visualization, with a focus on enabling educators to author and share accessible course content. My goal is to reduce the structural gaps created by inaccessible materials and build systems that allow people with disabilities to fully participate in learning, work, and everyday life.

My research has focused on designing and evaluating accessible representations of diagrams such as flowcharts and data structure diagrams. This work has resulted in projects including Arboretum, a system for accessible data structure diagrams in computer science education, and AccessFlow, a system that transforms flowchart images into accessible representations. AccessFlow was initially developed to support remediation—making existing diagrams accessible—but my current PhD work extends this effort to a broader challenge: enabling people to author diagrams nonvisually. This accessible authoring experience will be integrated into AccessFlow as the project evolves.

Creating new knowledge as an academic is rewarding, but I continually return to a core question: what is the point of understanding the potential of accessible diagrams and visualizations if that knowledge is not translated into systems people can use? This question drives my commitment to translating academic work into real-world tools and systems, ensuring that inclusive technologies move beyond research and into sustained use.
